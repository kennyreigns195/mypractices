Classic Nigerian Jollof Rice

A rich, smoky, and deeply flavourful one-pot rice dish that is the pride of Nigerian cuisine. This recipe delivers the iconic party jollof taste — perfectly seasoned, beautifully red, and impossible to resist.

Recipe Details

  • Prep Time: 20 minutes
  • Cook Time: 55 minutes
  • Servings: 6 people
  • Difficulty: Intermediate

Ingredients

  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice
  • 6 large ripe tomatoes, blended
  • 3 red bell peppers (tatashe), blended
  • 2 scotch bonnet peppers (atarodo), blended
  • 1 large onion, half blended and half sliced
  • ½ cup vegetable oil (or chicken fat for extra flavour)
  • 2 cups chicken stock (or water)
  • 1 teaspoon thyme
  • 1 teaspoon curry powder
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 seasoning cubes (e.g. Maggi or Knorr), to taste
  • Salt to taste

Instructions

  1. Blend the pepper base: Combine the tomatoes, red bell peppers, scotch bonnet, and half the onion in a blender. Blend until completely smooth and set aside.
  2. Fry the tomato paste: Heat the vegetable oil in a large, heavy-bottomed pot over medium-high heat. Add the sliced onions and fry for 2 minutes until softened. Pour in the blended pepper mix and fry, stirring occasionally, for 25–30 minutes until the paste darkens, thickens, and the oil rises to the top. This step is critical — do not rush it.
  3. Season the base: Add thyme, curry powder, bay leaves, seasoning cubes, and salt. Stir well and cook for another 2 minutes to bloom the spices.
  4. Add stock and rice: Pour in the chicken stock. Taste the liquid and adjust seasoning — it should be slightly saltier than you want the finished dish. Add the washed rice, stir once to combine, then level the surface.
  5. Steam on low heat: Cover the pot tightly with foil, then place the lid on top. Reduce heat to the lowest setting and cook for 25–30 minutes without lifting the lid. The steam trapped inside is what perfectly cooks the rice.
  6. Check and fluff: After 25 minutes, open carefully (watch the steam!). If the rice is cooked and all liquid is absorbed, remove from heat. If still firm, add 2–3 tablespoons of water, re-cover, and steam for 5 more minutes.
  7. Serve hot: Fluff with a fork and serve alongside fried plantain (dodo), moi moi, or grilled chicken.

Tips & Notes

  • The smoky bottom (party secret): For authentic party jollof, turn the heat to medium for the final 3 minutes. This creates a slightly charred, smoky base that is the hallmark of great Jollof Rice.
  • Always fry the tomato base until the raw smell is completely gone — this is the most common mistake beginners make.
  • Use a tight-fitting lid or double-wrap with foil for the best steam seal and fluffy results.
  • Parboiled rice works best for this recipe — it stays firm and absorbs flavour without turning mushy.
